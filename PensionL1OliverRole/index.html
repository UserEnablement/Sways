Contribution rates
A member calls Oliver's support team. They are confused because their pension contribution was higher this month than last month. They want to know why their rate changed.
Who sets contribution rates — and what is Oliver's role?
✅ Answer
The union and employer set the rate — through the collective agreement. Oliver did not change it.
Contribution rates are negotiated between the union and the employer and written into the collective agreement. When a new agreement comes into effect, rates may change. Oliver reads those rates from the agreement and applies them to each remittance — but it has no authority to set or modify them.
The distinction that matters Oliver applies the rate. The collective agreement sets the rate. If a member or employer has questions about why a rate changed, the answer comes from the plan administrator or the union — not from Oliver.

Investment performance
The pension fund lost 8% of its value in the markets this year. A DB plan member calls, worried that their monthly retirement payment will decrease because of the poor returns.
Who is responsible for making up the shortfall — and what does Oliver do?
✅ Answer
The employer (plan sponsor) carries the investment risk — not the member, and not Oliver.
In a Defined Benefit plan, the member is promised a specific monthly amount based on a formula — years of service, benefit rate, and average earnings. That promise does not change because markets dropped. If the fund's assets fall short of what is needed to pay those promised amounts, the employer must make up the difference. This is exactly what makes DB plans valuable to members: the investment risk is carried by the employer.
The distinction that matters Oliver calculates and records contributions and entitlements. It does not hold the investment portfolio, guarantee returns, or fund deficiencies. Investment management belongs to the custodian. Funding obligations belong to the employer.
Scenario 3 of 3 Click to reveal ↓
Pension payroll discrepancy
A retiree notices that their January pension payment was $12 less than their December payment. They call to find out why. The plan administrator wants to investigate.
What is Oliver's role here — and who makes the decision about what to do?
✅ Answer
Oliver processes pension payroll and holds the complete record. The plan administrator investigates and decides what action to take.
Oliver generates pension payroll — it calculates and issues the monthly payments based on the member's entitlement on record. If a payment amount changes unexpectedly, Oliver's records are the source of truth for investigating why: the payment history, any adjustments posted, the entitlement calculation. But Oliver does not decide what happened or what to do about it — that is the plan administrator's role. Oliver executes and records; the administrator reviews and decides.
The distinction that matters Oliver is the platform — it holds the data and runs the calculations. The plan administrator is the decision-maker — they use Oliver's data to investigate, communicate with the member, and direct any corrections. Oliver does not communicate directly with members about their payments.
